What the app sends, and what it doesn’t

Your logbook never leaves your Mac. The app reads a copy of it and does its work locally. LogTen Assistant has no account, no analytics and no advertising.

What the app contacts

  • Crash and diagnostic reports. Release builds send crash reports to Sentry so bugs can be found and fixed. They describe the app, not your logbook, and are redacted: no flight data, registrations, names or file paths.
  • Update checks. One request to GitHub’s public releases API, sending nothing about you.
  • FlySto. Only if you add an API token. Sync sends it to FlySto to fetch your logs. Data logs you choose to upload go to FlySto; nothing else does.
  • adsb.lol. When you fetch ADS-B data, the app downloads the public daily archives. Only the archive requests leave your Mac; your aircraft are picked out after they arrive.
  • OpenSky Network. Only if you add OpenSky credentials. They go to OpenSky’s sign-in service to authorize requests to its API.
  • FAA. NASR airport data and CIFP approach data are public downloads. The airmen database is downloaded only when you use Look Up…, and searched on your Mac.

What stays on your Mac

  • The logbook, its snapshots and backups.
  • Your review decisions, notes and the history of applied fixes, in a workspace folder the app keeps for each logbook.
  • Credentials, which are kept in the Keychain.

The full policy is on the website.

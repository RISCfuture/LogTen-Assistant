Why the app reads a copy of your logbook

LogTen may be writing to its logbook at any moment, and a sandboxed app can only read it. So LogTen Assistant never opens the live logbook at all. It works from a snapshot: a copy of the logbook’s files, made for the audit.

How a snapshot is made

  1. The app copies the logbook’s store and its journal files, using file coordination so LogTen isn’t caught mid-write.
  2. It compares each file’s size and modification date from before and after the copy. If anything changed while it copied, it copies again.
  3. On the copy only, it merges the journal into the store and runs SQLite’s integrity check.

Everything the app shows you — findings, violations, the flights themselves — comes from the snapshot. Choosing Audit ▸ Run Audit takes a new one.

When the logbook changes underneath you

If you edit in LogTen after an audit, the findings reflect the logbook as it was. Run the audit again to catch up. Before applying any fix, the app takes a fresh snapshot and checks each fix against it; a fix whose flight changed in the meantime is left out and listed instead. See Apply fixes to your logbook.

The app changes your logbook only through LogTen’s own import, and only when you apply fixes and choose to send them. Reading and auditing never write anything.
